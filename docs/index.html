Does the method work?

We re-ran the method on the eve of the 2016 and 2021 elections, using only data available then, and counted correct ward winners. Each province was held out while fitting.

How the forecast works

  1. New wards. The IEC lists the voting districts inside each 2026 ward (Municipal Demarcation Board boundaries). Past results for those voting districts are added up inside the new wards.
  2. History. For each party, a model links a ward's 2024 national-ballot share and its 2021 local ward-ballot share to the next local result. MK's share of the old ANC local vote follows each ward's 2024 ANC/MK split.
  3. By-elections. More than 200 ward by-elections since the 2024 election show where parties are running ahead of or behind their 2024 result, by province and community type. Wards get a light adjustment from these, at the strength that worked best in the 2016 and 2021 tests.
  4. Polls. Each party's overall level is set to a poll average that favours recent and larger polls and corrects for each pollster's lean in 2024. Metros with their own polls are pulled towards them.
  5. Seats. Council seats follow the local-government formula: ward and party votes combined, quota and largest remainders, ward winners first.
  6. Uncertainty. 2,000 simulations add poll error and municipal and ward-level surprises, sized from the 2021 test. A ward's chance is how often each party won it.

What it doesn't know

  • Who is standing. Ward candidate lists are not published as data; the model can use one if supplied, but this run does not.
  • Turnout. A turnout shock is simulated (parties whose voters turn out reliably gain when turnout falls), but its size is an assumption.
  • Census 2022 ward demographics are not released as data, so community types are built from voting patterns instead.
  • New parties without a 2021 or 2024 result are counted with "Other".

Independent model, not an official result. Not affiliated with the IEC, the Municipal Demarcation Board or any party. Forecasts are probabilities, and the less likely outcome will happen in some wards.
